# SCAR TISSUE — The Mirror Ward: Full Scene

*The emotional centre of the game (~12 minutes). Almost no danger. The horror is grief.*

**Resolves the open question from Ward 1:** the "fresh" signature in the ledger and the postcard in Mara's handwriting were written by **Nell**. The only letter she ever kept from Mara was the 1979 consent form. She learned to write by copying her sister's hand, and she has been signing Mara's name for fifteen years, hoping the building would carry it out into the world.

---

## Beat 1 — Arrival (the lit window)

**What the player sees:** The service stair opens onto the third floor through a swollen door. The ward runs away in front of them: sixty metres of lime-green plaster, forty iron beds in two rows, a tall angled mirror between every pair. At the far end, one tall window glows warm against the grey. The reflections multiply it down the length of the room, so the light seems to come from everywhere at once.

**Sound:** Rain on the roof. A low, soft hum, many voices on one note, felt more than heard. It rises slightly when Mara steps in.

**Mara (quiet):** *"The window. That's the light I saw from the road."*

**Direction:** The player is free to walk. There is no threat in the ward. The Understudy is nowhere in sight. The hum is the only sign of it.

## Beat 2 — The Drawings

Above each bed, crayon drawings pinned to the wall: houses, rivers, a sun with a face. Every third drawing is of **joined hands**, a chain of paper dolls. The paper is old. The newest drawings are on *new* paper, and they are of **two figures**, a tall one and a small one, holding hands. The tall one has dark hair and wears a long white coat.

**Mara (stopping at a drawing):** *"That's… a surgeon's coat. She drew me in a coat. I wasn't a doctor yet. I was seventeen."*

Under the picture, in a child's careful capitals: **MARA WILL COME.** Below that, a tally of small pencil strokes, hundreds of them, in rows of five.

## Beat 3 — The Mirrors

Walking down the ward, the player's reflection appears in each mirror, receding in both directions. As they pass, each mirror holds the reflection **half a second late**.

Then the wounds begin to show. Any injury the player is carrying appears *in the reflections first* before it can be seen on the body: a limp in the far mirror, a bandaged hand held up in a mirror to the left. Further down the ward the reflections begin to show wounds the player does **not** have, which belong to **other patients**: a split lip, a burned hand, a broken wrist set badly. Each reflection looks briefly at Mara, then away.

**Mara (barely audible):** *"They're not mine. They're… somebody else's. Why can I feel them looking?"*

## Beat 4 — The Graft Beds

At the centre of the ward the beds change: heavier frames, wide leather straps, brass ports running down into the floor. The floor has been cut open in a neat square. A shaft drops into darkness, and **warmth** rises from it, like breath. A brass plaque on the nearest bed:

> *THE PATIENT IS NEVER ALONE.*

**Mara:** *"Henn's. He wrote this."* She touches the strap. The leather is **warm**.

She kneels at the shaft. From far below, a faint rhythm: a heartbeat. Then a second, slightly out of step. Then a third.

## Beat 5 — Nell's Bed

At the far end, under the lit window, a small bed made up with a child's patchwork quilt. A hair ribbon, a milk tooth, a cracked hand mirror, a plaster cast of a child's left hand with a crescent scar, all lined up on the windowsill, evenly spaced. On the pillow, a postcard identical to the one Mara received, and beside it a pencil worn down to a stub and a **stack of consent forms**, all with Mara's signature copied hundreds of times, the handwriting improving from a child's scrawl to a perfect match.

**Mara (very quiet, picking up a sheet):** *"The first ones are… she was nine. These are just shapes. And here…"* She turns it over. *"This is mine. This is exactly mine."*

**Mara:** *"She wrote the postcard. She wrote the ledger. She's been… writing me. For fifteen years. The only thing she had of me was my name."*

## Beat 6 — The Tapes

In the little office behind the ward, a battered tape deck with a single reel threaded. Pressing play, the hiss of old magnetic tape, then a girl's voice, steady, calm, much too grown for nine.

> **NELL (age 9, tape):** *Tape four. It's Tuesday, I think. Dr. Henn says I can talk into this if I want to. He says it helps to say it out loud.*
>
> *It doesn't hurt as much now. The others take some of it. That's what the straps are for. I can feel them, a little, like when you hear someone in the next room. Mr. Alder has a bad leg. Sister Agnes hurts in her chest. Small Tom is scared of the dark.*
>
> *When I'm scared, they're not so scared. And when they're scared, I'm not so… It's like holding hands in the dark. That's what I think it is.*
>
> *Mara says she'll visit when she finishes school. She promised. I'm practicing my writing so I can write to her. She's got such nice handwriting. I'm going to write exactly like her.*
>
> *Okay. That's all. Tape stops now.*

A pause. The hiss. Then a **second, older voice**, the same voice, but soft with years, speaking over the tape in the present:

> **NELL (present, through the room):** *I'm still saying it out loud, Mara. I just don't need the tape.*

The hum rises. Every mirror in the ward brightens at once.

**Mara (barely able to speak):** *"Nell?"*

> **NELL:** *Don't be scared. It only hurts a bit. And it's not mine. It's… everyone's. Come down. They want to meet you.*

## Beat 7 — The Understudy Waits

When Mara turns, the Understudy stands at the end of the ward, in the doorway, framed by the grey light. For the first time it is perfectly still, not following, not mirroring, only looking. Its body bears *every wound the player is carrying*, laid out like a medical diagram. It lifts one hand slowly, palm open, the way a child offers a hand to cross a road.

**Mara:** *"It's… her. No. It's all of them."*

> **NELL:** *That's Tom. And Agnes. And Mr. Alder. They don't have a face anymore, but they know you. You hurt like me.*

The Understudy steps aside, clearing the doorway. The shaft in the floor glows faintly. The hum drops to a single sustained note, like a held breath.

**Direction:** The player may choose to approach the Understudy before leaving. If they do, it does not retreat. It places its open palm an inch from theirs and holds it there. The player's scars glow on both hands. A moment later it lowers its hand and *limps ahead of them toward the stair*, leading.

## Beat 8 — Exit

The only way onward is down the shaft, a spiral service stair around the warm dark. It descends floor after floor to the boiler house, whose heat is what rises through the shaft. (Tomas's radio call, which follows, sends her the rest of the way.) As Mara leaves, the lamp at the end of the ward goes out. In the dark mirrors, the player's reflection stands still for one second longer than it should, then follows.

**Closing line (Mara):** *"I'm coming, Nell. I'm sorry it took so long."*

---

## Tone Notes
- No jump scares in this ward. The scare is *recognition*.
- The score should be nearly absent. Let the hum, the rain and the tape hiss carry the scene.
- The player should feel pity for the Understudy for the first time here. This reframes every earlier chase.
