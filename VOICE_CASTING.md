# SCAR TISSUE — Voice Casting & Direction

*Casting briefs for each speaking role, with direction for how each voice should change across the story. Describes the **vocal profile to find**, not named performers.*

**Global direction**
- Naturalistic, low-volume, close-mic. Nobody shouts for effect. The game's fear lives in restraint.
- Record dry (no reverb) so the audio team can place every voice in 3D space.
- Mara's lines are **spoken to herself**, rarely to others. Record every line at three levels: *thought* (barely voiced), *murmur*, and *speaking*, so the game can scale delivery to the player's current stress.
- Provide intimacy takes for every voiced line (mouth very close to the mic) and plain takes for loudspeaker, radio and tape playback, which are processed in-engine.

---

## 1. Dr. Mara Voss (lead, 32)

**Profile:** Female, low-to-mid register, dry and exact diction. A surgeon's habit of speaking in short, complete phrases. Warmth is present but kept under control.

**What to find:** A performer who can play *competent grief*: a person who handles pressure well and hasn't forgiven herself. Must carry long stretches alone with almost no one to talk to.

**Arc of the voice**
| Stage | Delivery |
|---|---|
| Ward 1–2 | Controlled, wry, clipped. Counts things out loud. |
| Ward 3 | Colder, clinical when she is frightened; her expertise shows. |
| Ward 4 | Breaks. Voice thins, pauses lengthen, sentences end unfinished. |
| Ward 5–6 | Steadier but softer. Speaks *to* people instead of *about* them. |
| Endings | Severance: tired and tender. Vessel: calm, almost smiling. Release: quiet, assured, hands-steady voice. |

**Special sessions**
- **Pain vocalisation set:** non-verbal sounds for each zone and severity (sharp breath, held groan, bitten-back cry, laboured breathing), recorded in a medical-safe session with a vocal coach. These are heavily used and must feel *human*, not heroic.
- **Treatment mutters:** short self-talk while bandaging and splinting (*"Over, under, tuck."*), ~40 variations so repeats don't wear thin.
- **Counting:** numbers 1–100 in calm and in strained delivery.

## 2. Nell Voss

Two performers, because she is heard at two ages.

### 2a. Nell, age 9 (tapes)
**Profile:** A real child, ideally 9–11, with a calm, careful voice. Not "creepy-child" acting. She sounds older than her years because she has had to be. Slight breathiness; gentle humour.
**Direction:** She is explaining things matter-of-factly to a tape recorder. She is *not* scared. The horror is in how normal this is to her.
**Production notes:** Child-performer protections apply: limited session length, a guardian present, a licensed chaperone, and age-appropriate script review. Record the tape lines in a quiet, informal setting. Process in-engine for tape age (wow/flutter, hiss).

### 2b. Nell, age 24 (present)
**Profile:** Female, soft and clear, light in the mid register. A little hoarse from disuse.
**Direction:** Serene, not otherworldly. She speaks as someone who has lived inside a very large feeling for a long time. When the Chorus speaks *through* her, her voice carries a faint doubling.
**Layered voice:** Record her normal voice for the single-voice passages, then a second layer of whispered, breathy delivery of the same lines, to be mixed under her normal voice in the Chorus Room (see Sound Design).

## 3. Tomas Ilves (radio and in person, ~58)

**Profile:** Male, low-mid, worn, gentle. A trace of an Estonian or Baltic accent (his surname is Estonian; the accent should be light and real, from a performer who speaks natively or with accent coaching).
**Direction:** Tired, courteous, ashamed. He speaks quietly, like someone who has talked to himself for thirty years. He never raises his voice, even in the collapse.

**Two modes**
- **Radio:** compressed, crackling, the voice leaning toward the mic.
- **In person:** drier, fuller, slower.

**Key scenes**
- First radio call (Ward 2): guarded, careful. Warns without pleading.
- Confession (Ward 5): long, uninterrupted, almost without performance. A real, human cadence matters more than emotion.
- Collapse: urgent but still polite (*"Go! Go down!"*).
- Last radio line if left behind: very quiet, nearly an exhale.

---

## 4. Dr. Aldric Henn (recorded, ~60 in 1961)

**Profile:** Male, warm baritone, an educated, slightly archaic mid-century English, with the soft consonants of a man trained to calm patients.
**Direction:** He believes he is doing good. The audio should never signal "villain". He is tender and increasingly obsessed, so the listener sympathises before they are horrified.

**Recording notes**
- Process in-engine as reel-to-reel: narrow bandwidth, tape hiss, gentle wow.
- The last tape (flood night) is recorded closer, breathier, with the sound of water nearby (foley layer, not a studio effect).

---

## 5. The Chorus (non-verbal and chorus voices)

**Profile:** A mixed ensemble of 12–20 voices of all ages and genders: a few children, many adults, a couple of elders.
**What they record**
- **The hum:** sustained single-note humming on a shared pitch (see Music).
- **Whispered words:** *Hello. You hurt. We know. We hurt too. Stay. Thank you.*, each recorded individually by every voice, then layered.
- **Breath and heartbeat set:** slow collective breathing, staggered.
- **Ambient fragments:** half-heard names (*Tom, Agnes, Alder*), laughter, a lullaby, all low in the mix.

**Direction:** Gentle, not eerie. Singers should imagine *comforting someone in the dark*. No growling, no distortion.

---

## 6. The Understudy (non-verbal)

No dialogue. Vocal material is **breath only**, recorded from a few performers (including the Chorus ensemble) and designed in-engine to double the player's breath at a fraction's delay.

## 8. Session Plan
1. **Pre-production:** voice tests for Mara/Nell age-match; Tomas accent coaching; child-performer approval.
2. **Principal recording:** Mara (longest), Tomas, Henn, adult Nell, in roughly that order.
3. **Child sessions:** Nell age 9, short sessions, spread over several days.
4. **Ensemble day:** Chorus recorded as a group *and* individually.
5. **Pickups:** Mara pain vocalisations and treatment mutters in a dedicated session.
6. **Localisation:** record at least English, plus text for subtitles in major languages. Preserve the *quiet* delivery style in any dubbing. Subtitles are the default for tapes and radio.

## 9. Accessibility
- **All spoken lines have captions**, including non-verbal cues in square brackets (*[Chorus hum rises]*).
- Radio, tape and Chorus lines have **transcripts in the Documents Binder**.
- Mix keeps dialogue legible: a dedicated dialogue duck on score and ambience, optional "dialogue boost" setting.
