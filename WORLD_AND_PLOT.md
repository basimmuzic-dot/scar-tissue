# SCAR TISSUE — World & Plot

*What the player sees, ward by ward, and the story those places tell. No systems talk here, only place, atmosphere and plot.*

---

# PART I — THE PLACE

## 1. St. Aldric Sanatorium: The Whole Building

**Where:** A river valley in the mountains, November 1994. Grey light, constant fine rain, mist lying on the water. The nearest town is forty minutes back down a washed-out road.

**What it is:** A long Victorian-era hospital, four storeys of pale stone with a slate roof, built in 1897 for "rest cures" and extended in the 1950s with a flat-roofed concrete wing. It sits on a shelf of rock above a river that has since climbed its banks. The newer wing is already sinking: tide-lines of brown water mark every wall at knee height, then waist height, then ceiling height as you descend.

**The key idea for the player's eye:** the building is a **vertical gradient of water and light**. The upper floors are dry, rotting and pale. The middle floors are damp, sodden and green. The lowest floors are submerged, black and warm. The deeper you go, the less natural light, the warmer the air feels, and the more the walls look like *living tissue* instead of plaster.

### Cross-section (top to bottom)
```
ROOF & ATTIC ........ collapsed slate, open sky, pigeons, rain falling through
FLOOR 3: Wards ...... iron beds, peeling lime-green paint, dry, silent
FLOOR 2: Surgical ... tiled theatres, glass galleries, brass fittings
GROUND: Admissions .. marble lobby, records, brown tide-line at the knee
BASEMENT 1: Baths ... hydrotherapy halls, half flooded, tiled blue-white
BASEMENT 2: Boiler .. brick, soot, iron, burned out, heat still in the walls
SUB-LEVEL: Foundations  flooded stone vaults, black water, nerve-roots in the walls
```

### The building's colour language
| Zone | Dominant colours | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| Upper floors | Bone white, lime green, rust orange | Faded institutional calm |
| Surgical | Cold teal tile, brass, glass | Clinical, watched |
| Baths | Pool blue, white, slick black | Eerie beauty |
| Boiler | Soot black, ember orange | Oppression, heat |
| Foundations | Wet black, bruise purple, pale pink-grey | Organic, alive |

### The one recurring motif
**Hands.** Handprints in dust, a plaster cast of a hand in a case, gloves hung to dry, a child's hand drawn on a wall. And **circles**: a wedding-ring, a crescent scratch, the rims of baths, the mirrors. Whenever the player sees a circle or a hand, they are being told something about Nell.

---

## 2. The Approach (Opening)

The player stands at the end of a flooded drive. A pair of iron gates hang open, one slumped into the mud. The drive is lined with dead poplars, their trunks dark with rain. Through the mist, the sanatorium rises: pale, symmetrical, its central clock tower stopped at 2:47. Dozens of tall windows, most dark, a few broken. In **one third-floor window a light is on.** It does not flicker. It never explains itself.

Mara's car sits behind, engine ticking as it cools, wipers still. Ahead, the double doors stand slightly ajar. Rain drums on the portico roof. A faded brass plate by the door: *ST. ALDRIC — A RETREAT FOR THE RECOVERY OF THE NERVES.*

---

## 3. Ward 1 — Admissions

**Mood:** a grand place gone quiet. Beautiful and wrong.

### Entrance Hall
A double-height marble lobby, black-and-white chequered floor buckled by water. A grand staircase curves up into darkness, the lower treads swollen and furred with green. A ceiling section has collapsed, so a column of grey daylight falls straight to the floor, lighting a drift of plaster dust, a toppled wheelchair, and a single pair of leather shoes standing neatly side by side. A chandelier hangs one chain from falling. The air smells of wet stone and old paper. The brown **tide-line sits at the knee** on every wall, with a crust of pale mineral above it.

### Reception
A long polished desk with a cracked glass partition above it. A brass bell, a rack of numbered room keys (most hooks empty), a corkboard of pinned notices curling in the damp. Behind the glass, a ledger lies open under a desk lamp whose bulb is long dead. Dust lies thick everywhere except one place: a clear patch where a hand was recently resting.

### Records Room
Rows of tall steel filing cabinets, drawers hanging open like jaws, paper spilling to the floor in drifts. One aisle is *tidy*: drawers closed, labels straight. The dust on the floor holds a trail of narrow footprints leading in and none leading out. At the end of the aisle, a green banker's lamp lies on its side. A single cabinet drawer is open, labelled **V**.

### Staff Corridor
A long, narrow corridor with a linoleum floor the colour of old mustard. Doors on both sides: *Dispensary, Linen, Night Sister, Administrator.* The last is locked. Water drips steadily from the ceiling into a pail. At the far end, a fire door with a wire-glass panel, light shimmering green through it.

### Waiting Room (side room)
Rows of wooden chairs, all facing the same wall, where a framed photo of the staff hangs. Every face in the photograph has been scratched out except two: a girl of about nine, and a young man in an orderly's coat (Tomas).

### Stairwell
A spiral of iron and stone, descending in damp, echoing circles. The handrail is sticky with moisture. A faint blue-white glow rises from below, and the **sound of water, moving**.

### What the player should feel
Hushed, a little grand, and quietly watched. Nothing moves. Everything is *arranged*.

---

## 4. Ward 2 — Hydrotherapy (The Baths)

**Mood:** a cathedral of water. Gorgeous and dangerous.

### Stair Landing & Locker Hall
The stairs end at a landing lit by emergency lamps, still on, glowing sick amber. Beyond is a long hall lined with rows of dented green lockers, doors hanging, each stencilled with a number. Wet footprints cross the floor, some bare and small. A line of rubber bathing caps hangs like skins on a rail. The ceiling pipes weep rusty water; a rhythmic **drip, drip, drip** is the room's heartbeat.

### Baths A (Shallow Hall)
A vast tiled hall, white and powder-blue, with a high vaulted ceiling of glass tiles. A row of **copper-rimmed circular baths** sit sunken in the floor, a metre of still water in each, the surface perfectly flat. Water also covers the main floor to ankle-depth. Steam does not rise; instead, a fine mist hangs. Light comes from above through filthy skylights, falling in long pale shafts onto the water. Reflections show the vault again and again, so the hall looks twice as tall. Wooden duckboards, slick with algae, form narrow dry paths.

Along the far wall, at the edge of the water, stand **iron treatment frames**: tall, ring-shaped devices with leather straps, like medieval apparatus rendered clean and modern.

### Pump Room (side space)
A cramped brick chamber, humid and loud. Huge riveted pumps sit rusted and silent. A metal grating forms the floor, with black water rushing visibly *beneath* your feet. A steel medical cabinet is bolted to the wall, its glass door intact: the only tidy thing in the room.

### Catwalk Level
Above the baths runs a long **iron gantry**, three metres over the water, built for staff to watch the patients. Its handrails are flaking, bolts weeping rust. Sections sag. One stretch has simply fallen away, leaving a four-metre gap with torn girders curling over the void. From up here the baths below look like a ring of eyes.

### Baths B (Deep Hall)
The floor falls away into a **deep pool**, black and wide, its surface rippling slightly though nothing is moving. A narrow ledge runs around the edge, wet and treacherous. Under the surface, pale tile glimmers far down, and *something white and thin* that might be a rope or a handrail. On the far side, a brass-gated service lift waits, its lamp glowing faintly.

### The Lift
Brass walls, polished by decades of hands, a folding gate, and a single wall-mounted mirror clouded with age. Reflected in it, the player sees themselves... and sometimes a second figure standing just behind, out of focus.

### What the player should feel
Beauty first, then wrongness. The water is too still. The reflections are always a little late.

---

## 5. Ward 3 — The Surgical Wing

**Mood:** cold precision, being observed.

Reached by the brass lift, up and out onto the second floor, the air changes: dry, chemical, faintly sweet.

### Corridors
White-tiled floors and teal-tiled walls, laid with surgical neatness. The ceiling lights are caged glass bulbs, half dead, and the working ones buzz. A faint green exit sign glows at every corner. Gurneys stand parked in a row, their sheets perfectly smooth.

### Operating Theatre 1 (the main safe space)
A round amphitheatre with a central **operating table under a great overhead lamp**, a ring of enormous reflective discs. Tiered rows of wooden seats rise around it, behind a curved glass gallery. Instruments are laid out on a steel tray as if the surgeon just stepped away: scalpels in rows, curved needles, thread, a kidney dish. It is the cleanest, most orderly room in the building. A radiator ticks. A chair has been set beside the table: someone *has been sitting here*.

### The Gallery
Above the theatre, a darkened glass-walled observation space. The player can stand here and look *down* at the table. From above, the table's lamp reflects as a pale circle on the dark glass, and the player's own reflection stands in it, tiny.

### Instrument Store
Narrow shelves of brown glass bottles, labelled in Latin and in Henn's neat hand. Rows of bone saws, retractors, clamps hanging from hooks like trophies. One cabinet is lined with **jars of preserved nerve tissue**, grey-pink threads suspended in clear fluid. Each jar is labelled with a *name*, and each name is a patient.

### Henn's Office (unlocked later)
Dark wood panelling, a green leather desk, a wall of framed diplomas. Behind the desk, a huge **anatomical drawing of the human nervous system** hangs, overlaid with hand-drawn lines connecting *figures* instead of organs. A reel-to-reel tape recorder sits on the desk with a single tape threaded.

### What the player should feel
Order that has become horror. The place is *too* clean, and the instruments are *too* ready.

---

## 6. Ward 4 — The Mirror Ward

**Mood:** dreamlike, intimate, devastating.

Up a service stair to the third floor, to the one lit window from the opening.

### The Long Dormitory
A very long, narrow ward, perhaps sixty metres, with a high ceiling and tall windows down one side. Forty iron beds in two rows, each made up with grey blankets. Between every pair of beds stands a **tall angled mirror**, set so that, looking along the ward, you see the reflection of a reflection of a reflection, receding forever. No bed can see only itself. The light comes from the windows, soft and grey, and from a single lamp at the far end.

The walls are lime-green plaster, peeling in long curls to show older layers: cream, then pink, then bare brick. Children's drawings are pinned above the beds, mostly of **hands joined in chains**, rendered in crayon. Some drawings have been added to *recently*, in handwriting that matches Mara's own.

### Graft Beds
At the ward's centre, a cluster of beds is different: heavier, with wide leather restraints and slim brass **ports and tubes** running from the bed frames toward the floor. The floor beneath them has been cut open in a neat square, revealing a *shaft* that drops into darkness and from which a faint, steady **warmth** rises. Beside the shaft, a small brass plaque: *"The patient is never alone."*

### Nell's Bed (the one by the lit window)
A small bed at the far end, made up with a child's quilt. On the pillow, a **paper postcard** matching the one Mara received. On the windowsill, a row of tiny objects arranged with care: a hair ribbon, a milk tooth, a cracked hand mirror, and a **plaster cast of a child's left hand** with the crescent scar.

### The Playback Room
A little office at the ward's end, with a battered tape deck. The tapes inside, recordings of Nell, age nine, speaking softly: *"The others hurt with me. It's okay. They're not so scared when it's shared."*

### What the player should feel
Gentle and unbearable. The ward is not frightening in a loud way. It is a place where someone was *loved and failed*.

---

## 7. Ward 5 — The Boiler House

**Mood:** heat, guilt, labour.

Reached by the spiral service stair that winds down the Mirror Ward's warm shaft, past the dark floors, to the building's deepest working level. The warmth rising in the shaft is the boilers' heat.

### The Furnace Hall
A cavernous space of **blackened brick** and iron, forty feet high. Three vast boilers sit in a row, riveted steel as large as railway engines, still faintly warm. Soot is everywhere, lying thick and soft on every surface, so the player's footprints and handprints stay visible. Orange light comes from a single boiler whose firebox door is open and still holds a low, **steady glow** that has no right to be burning. Heat bends the air.

### The Coal Store
A sloping chamber of black dunes, a conveyor frozen mid-run, a wheelbarrow still full. Among the coal lie scattered **personal objects**: spectacles, a shoe, a rosary, a child's mitten. They are not buried; they were *swept aside*.

### Tomas's Quarters
A tiny, painfully tidy room carved out of the brick wall: a cot, a paraffin stove, a shelf of canned food, a battered radio wired to an aerial that runs up through the ceiling. The walls are covered with **hundreds of pencil tally marks**, in dense columns. A photo of the staff, the same as in the Waiting Room, but with all the faces *intact*.

### The Collapse
A gallery of rotted beams above a flooded pit. When the fire wing finally gives way, the floor drops into the dark water, and the whole hall groans like a living animal.

### What the player should feel
Heat, sweat, and a human presence. This is the first place where the building feels *inhabited*, and the first place the player meets another person.

---

## 8. Ward 6 — The Foundations (The Chorus Room)

**Mood:** awe and grief.

### The Descent
A rough stone stairway carved into the bedrock, running down into water. At the bottom, the walls change. The stone is overgrown with **pale, root-like cords**, thin as hair and thick as arms, running in bundles along walls and ceiling and pulsing, slowly, in a rhythm that matches the player's own heartbeat. The air is warm and damp, smelling faintly of salt and iron.

### The Vaults
A series of low, rounded stone chambers, knee-deep in black water. Light comes from nothing visible. The cords glow faintly from within, a bruised violet. In the water below, hundreds of small ripples travel outward from the player's feet, and the **surfaces answer**: the ripples return a moment later, slightly changed.

Niches in the walls hold **hundreds of faces**, softly formed into the cords like death masks: patients, men, women, children. They are not decaying. They are *resting*. Their mouths move silently, together.

### The Chorus Room
The final chamber is a vast domed cavern, perhaps fifty metres across, its ceiling lost in mist. The entire space is lined with the nerve-growth, forming a living **cathedral of pale tissue**. A broad, shallow pool fills the floor, perfectly black, and in its centre stands a **single platform of stone**: a bed.

On it lies **Nell**, twenty-four, thin, serene, wearing a hospital gown too old for her. Hundreds of fine cords rise from her body and stream into the walls, as if her hair had turned to roots. When she breathes, the whole room *breathes with her*; the glow brightens, then dims.

Above her, the stopped clock of the tower appears, impossible and distant, as if seen through water: **2:47**, the moment the fire began.

### What the player should feel
Sacred dread. This is not a monster's lair. It is a *shrine*.

# PART II — THE PLOT

## 9. Backstory (what happened)

**1961.** Dr. Aldric Henn, a brilliant, gentle, and quietly obsessed neurologist, believes chronic pain is not a fault in one body but a burden that can be *shared*. In the sanatorium's third-floor ward, he performs nerve-grafting surgeries that link patients' nervous systems, so each person's pain is divided among all. It works, terribly well. The patients grow calm. They begin to hum together. Henn calls them **the Chorus**.

**1962.** The river floods. The night of the rising water, a short circuit in the boiler house starts a fire. The staff evacuate the upper floors; the doors to the lower wards are chained shut by a young orderly, **Tomas Ilves**, who believes the patients are already dead or beyond saving. The fire burns for two days. Forty-one patients are recorded as dead. The building is sealed.

**What really happened.** The patients *did not die*. The graft meant that, as one body failed, its pain and awareness flowed into the others. As the group died, what remained concentrated into the deep nerve-tissue spreading through the foundations. By the time the flood retreated, the Chorus was a single awareness, patient, vast, and alone, feeling only what it had learned to feel: **pain.** It cannot see or speak. It has learned the world through anything that hurts near it.

**1979.** Nine-year-old **Nell Voss** is admitted after a fall left her with nerve pain no doctor can ease. Her seventeen-year-old sister **Mara**, newly her guardian after their parents' deaths, signs the consent forms for a "pain program" run by a successor clinic. In truth, Henn's old methods have been quietly revived in the sealed lower wards, and Nell becomes the Chorus's only living node, its first new voice in seventeen years.

**1994.** Mara is a respected trauma surgeon. She has never forgiven herself, and never found Nell, who she was told had *died of complications.* A postcard arrives in her own handwriting, postmarked from St. Aldric, bearing a photograph of a hand with a crescent scar.

---

## 10. The Story, Scene by Scene

### Act I: The Invitation
Mara drives through rain to the sanatorium. At the gate, she sees the single lit window. She goes in.

**Admissions.** She finds her own signature on Nell's admission, written in ink that is *wet*. A child's footprint trail leads across the lobby floor and stops at the stairwell. She hears, once, a very faint humming. Then the drip stops, and the entire building goes silent as if it were *holding its breath*.

**The Baths.** A radio crackles. A tired, kind voice tells her to leave. It is Tomas, who has lived in the boiler house for thirty years, keeping the Chorus warm and calm. Across the water she sees a figure that limps when she limps, that raises its hand when she raises hers. She flees across the catwalk, the whole structure shuddering, and the figure moves beneath her like a shadow cast the wrong way. When the lights surge, it stops, tilts its head, and *looks at the light.* It is not angry. It is *curious.*

In the lift, the mirror shows her, and behind her, the figure. It carries every wound she has.

### Act II: The Doctor
**The Surgical Wing.** Mara finds Henn's records and, with growing horror, recognises that the surgical techniques are *brilliant*, and that she herself uses them. Henn's marginal notes grow warmer, more tender, then desperate: *"They are not suffering. They are not alone."* On his last tape, recorded the night of the flood, he says: *"I have linked myself. I will not leave them."* He is the Chorus's first voice, and its conscience.

The figure follows her through the theatres, always a half-step behind. In the gallery, she sees it standing in the operating lamp's glow, mirroring her. It is *copying her scars.* She understands: **it is not copying her. It is feeling her.**

**The Mirror Ward.** The emotional centre of the game. Mara finds Nell's bed, the postcard, the plaster cast, and the recordings. Nell's child-voice explains what it was like: *"It hurts less when it's shared. But then I can feel all of them."* Mara learns that Nell survived. She is *still here.* And Mara's guilt, her long self-punishing certainty that she killed her sister, breaks open, because the truth is both better and worse.

The mirrors begin showing the player the *wounds they carry*, repeated down the ward in a hundred reflections. The figure stops chasing. It stands at the end of the ward. It is watching, and, for the first time, it does not move when she does.

### Act III: The Keeper
**The Boiler House.** Mara reaches Tomas. He is old now, frightened, and fiercely protective. He confesses: he chained the doors in 1962, believing it was mercy, and has spent thirty years feeding the boilers to keep the Chorus *warm*, because the one time he let the fires die it screamed, and every nerve in the building screamed with it. He begs her to leave Nell where she is. **The Chorus can't survive without a living node, and Nell can't survive without the Chorus.**

The floor collapses. Mara must choose: save Tomas, at a terrible cost to her own body, or press on alone.

**The Descent.** Down the stone steps, through the vaults of faces, to the Chorus Room. Every scar Mara has earned is now audible as a note in the room's sound. It is *playing her.* At the centre, Nell opens her eyes.

### The Final Scene
Nell is not a prisoner and not a victim. She is the Chorus's translator, and she speaks in two voices at once: her own, and the murmur of forty-one others. She says:

> "You came. I knew you would, because you were the only one who ever hurt the way I did. They like you. They've been trying to find you for so long."

She doesn't ask to be saved. She asks Mara what she wants the end of the story to be.

## 11. The Three Endings (as they look and feel)

### Severance — "Operate"
Mara performs the surgery she was trained for, cutting the final nerve-thread that binds Nell to the Chorus. The lights in the room *fade one by one*, like candles. The faces in the walls smile, and close their eyes. Nell gasps, takes a breath of her own, and cries. She is free, but Mara feels the loss of the Chorus's pain *pour into her*, a permanent ache across her whole body. At dawn, they walk out through the flooded drive, Nell leaning on Mara. Mara limps, and does not mind.

### Vessel — "Stay"
Mara lies on the platform beside her sister and takes the Chorus's pain into herself. The nerve-cords turn toward her, soft as breath. Nell sits up, breathing freely for the first time in fifteen years, and Mara closes her eyes. The flood in the lower wards recedes. The window on the third floor goes dark. In the final image, the sanatorium stands in clean morning light, silent and *peaceful*, and a figure moves slowly behind a window on the top floor, raising one hand in greeting.

### Release — "Let go"
(Earned only by caring for wounds, hers and the Understudy's, throughout the game.)
Mara does not cut and does not take. She sits with her sister and *comforts* the Chorus as she has learned to comfort herself, with steady hands, a calm voice, and patience. The cords loosen, softly, around Nell. The faces in the walls whisper something, together, that sounds like *thank you.* The glow dims, then fades into ordinary darkness. Nell wakes up, whole. The Chorus is *at rest.* Mara leaves the sanatorium, her scars faded, her hands finally steady, and does not look back.

## 12. Recurring Visual Beats (Reference for Art Direction)

1. **The stopped clock at 2:47**, seen in the tower, in a nurse's watch, and finally in the Chorus Room.
2. **Reflections that arrive late**: mirrors, baths, brass, glass.
3. **Circles**: bath rims, lamp discs, crescent scars, the Chorus Room's dome.
4. **Hands**: prints in dust, soot, mist on glass, children's drawings.
5. **A single warm light in a cold place**: the third-floor window, the open firebox, the glow of the cords.
6. **Water behaving wrongly**: too still, too warm, answering the player's footsteps.
