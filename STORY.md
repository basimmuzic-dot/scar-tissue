# SCAR TISSUE — Story Bible

*Companion to `GDD.md`. Written so every plot beat is delivered through a 3D space, a body state, or a sound, not through cutscenes alone.*

---

## 1. Logline

In 1994, trauma surgeon Mara Voss returns to the flooded ruin of St. Aldric Sanatorium to find her sister Nell, the patient she once signed away. Inside, she learns the building's dead were bound into one shared nervous system, and that whatever stalks her is not imitating pain. It is *feeling* hers.

## 2. Themes
- **Pain as inheritance:** what we do to others stays in the body, theirs and ours.
- **Consent and guilt:** a signature can be a wound.
- **Adaptation vs. cure:** some harm can't be undone, only lived with.

---

## 3. Backstory (revealed through the environment)

**1961 — Dr. Aldric Henn's "Sympathetic Therapy."** Henn believed chronic pain could be relieved by distributing it. Through experimental nerve-grafting surgery performed in the *Mirror Ward*, patients were linked so that one patient's pain was shared across the group, diluted among them. It seemed to work. Patients grew calm. Records called it "the Chorus."

**1962 — The Flood and Fire.** The river broke its banks. Staff evacuated, and the lower wards were sealed with patients still inside. The boiler house burned. Official record: 41 deaths, accident. The sanatorium closed.

**The truth:** The patients did not die separately. The link kept them, and their shared nerve tissue persisted in the flooded foundations as a single awareness, **the Chorus**. It cannot see well or speak. It only *feels*, and it learned the world through anything that hurt near it. It has been alone with all that pain for thirty-two years.

**Nell Voss** was admitted in 1979, age 9, after a fall that left her with chronic nerve pain. Mara, then a 17-year-old guardian after their parents' deaths, signed Henn's successor's consent forms for an "experimental pain program." Nell was the last patient linked, and the Chorus's only living node.

**Present, 1994:** Mara, now a surgeon, receives a postcard in her own handwriting, postmarked from the sanatorium, with a photograph of a hand bearing Nell's childhood scar. She goes.

## 4. Characters

**Dr. Mara Voss (player)** — Precise, controlled, privately guilty. Her skills (steady hands, anatomical knowledge) are the player's treatment mechanics. Her flaw: she believes every harm can be repaired if she is careful enough. Her arc ends in acceptance.

**Nell Voss** — Alive, now 24, kept in the foundations as the Chorus's living node. Not a villain, not a damsel. She is the Chorus's *translator*: its only link to speech. Her pain is the Chorus's pain.

**The Chorus ("The Understudy")** — The antagonist as the player first experiences it: a figure assembled from borrowed postures. It mimics injuries because it feels yours. When you limp, it limps; when you bleed, it bleeds; it follows you because you are the first *new pain* it has felt in decades. It is not hunting out of malice. It is drawn to you.

**Tomas Ilves** — Radio voice. A former orderly who survived the fire by sealing the lower wards. He is guilty, secretive, and the only other living person inside. He wants Mara to *leave*, because he knows what the Chorus does when it feels a new wound.

**Dr. Aldric Henn** — Absent. Present only through case files, recordings, and surgical notes with increasingly self-justifying margin comments. His final recording reveals he linked himself to the Chorus first.

## 5. Structure: Six Wards

Each ward is one 3D environment with a distinct injury theme and its own story reveal (it matches the GDD onboarding ladder).

### Ward 1 — Admissions (Tutorial / Atmosphere)
- **Space:** Water-stained lobby, reception, records room, a locked stairwell.
- **Story:** Mara finds her own signature on Nell's admission forms. The postcard's twin is pinned to the wall.
- **Mechanics:** Lacerations only (broken glass, rusted fittings). Learn bandaging.
- **Set piece:** A flooded corridor where the *sound* of dripping stops, the first sign something is listening.

### Ward 2 — Hydrotherapy (Flooded Wing)
- **Space:** Half-submerged tile baths, slick steps, rotted catwalks.
- **Story:** First contact with Tomas over a hand radio. He tells her to go back. First sighting of the Understudy, mirroring Mara's limp from across the water.
- **Mechanics:** Leg injuries and noise. Wading is silent, but broken tile is loud; a hurt leg changes which routes are viable.
- **Set piece:** Forced chase over a collapsing catwalk. Jumping the gap is faster but risks a fracture.

### Ward 3 — The Surgical Wing
- **Space:** Operating theatres, observation galleries, instrument stores.
- **Story:** Henn's case files. Mara realizes the experiments were *surgical*, and recognizes her own technique in his notes. His methods are *good surgery*, and that disturbs her.
- **Mechanics:** Sensory injuries (ears, eyes). The safe-room theatre appears here. First hard-surgery optional challenge.
- **Set piece:** A blackout in the gallery. Her flashlight is blinded by a burn, and she navigates by the *sound of the Understudy's matching footsteps*.

### Ward 4 — The Mirror Ward
- **Space:** Long dormitory with angled mirrors set so that no patient could see only themselves. Nerve-graft surgical beds.
- **Story:** The central reveal: Nell was linked. Mara hears recordings of Nell, age 9, describing "the others hurting with me." The Understudy stops chasing and *watches* her.
- **Mechanics:** The entity shows what *she* has feel: injuries on Mara echo on screen as faint ghost-injuries on mirrored figures, which the player can read to predict where it will strike.
- **Set piece:** A silent-stalking sequence where the player must stay *completely still* while bleeding, since the Understudy responds to pain, not sight.

### Ward 5 — Boiler House (The Fire Wing)
- **Space:** Burned brick, collapsed floors, furnace halls.
- **Story:** Tomas's confession. He sealed the lower wards, knowing patients were still alive. He has been feeding the boilers to *keep the Chorus warm and calm*. He's been caretaking it out of guilt.
- **Mechanics:** Burns and fire hazards, plus complications. Infections from earlier untreated wounds come due here, so past choices matter.
- **Set piece:** The player may choose to rescue Tomas from a collapse (costing her a serious injury) or leave him (costing the story's only living ally).

### Ward 6 — The Foundations (The Chorus Room)
- **Space:** Flooded vaults, root-like nerve growth threading the walls, a vast chamber where the patients' tissue lines the room. Nell lies at its centre.
- **Story:** Nell speaks through the Chorus. She isn't asking to be saved. She asks Mara to *stay* or to *finish it*.
- **Mechanics:** Everything converges. Every scar Mara carries is now audible as a distinct note in the Chorus's sound. The room's difficulty is set by how she's treated her body.

## 6. Endings (Shaped by Play, Not Dialogue Choice)

The ending is determined by the player's **treatment philosophy** across the game, tracked silently through three behaviours:

1. **Severance — "Operate."** Mara performs the final surgery to cut Nell from the Chorus. Success depends on her accumulated surgical skill. Nell is freed and the Chorus goes silent, but Mara carries *Nell's pain permanently* as a final scar. *Bittersweet.*
2. **Vessel — "Stay."** Mara accepts a link to carry the Chorus's pain, relieving Nell. They both survive, joined, and the building's flooding recedes. She never leaves. *Tragic but peaceful.*
3. **Release — "Let go."** Only available if Mara has treated wounds with care and compassion (not shortcutting). She guides the Chorus to stillness without surgery. Nell and the Chorus end together. Mara leaves alone, scarred but whole. *Hardest to earn, most hopeful.*

**New Game+ twist:** Scars carry over. The Understudy now *recognizes* the player; some encounters play differently because it remembers.

## 7. 3D Production Notes

- **Environment kit:** A modular set of hospital pieces (tile, plaster, iron, glass), reused across wards with different damage states (flooded, burned, overgrown).
- **Lighting is the primary storytelling tool:** the flashlight is a gameplay and narrative object. Wards are mostly dark with practical light sources. Visual contrast guides attention, with no UI arrows.
- **Water system:** Shallow/deep water that changes sound propagation. It is a core stealth mechanic and a unique visual signature.
- **Body presentation:** First-person hands and legs visible at all times, so the player reads their own injuries in the world. This is the single most important animation set (~12 wound-state variants per limb).
- **Audio:** Binaural 3D audio is mandatory. The Chorus's voice is built from layered breaths and heartbeats recorded as a "choir of bodies." Ear injuries *change the mix itself*.
- **The Understudy:** A modular character rig that can *mirror* the player's gait (procedural animation reading player limp/posture data). It never uses a fixed walk cycle.
- **Scope:** Six wards at roughly 90-minute play each, about 10–12 hours total. A vertical slice would be Wards 1–2 plus one Ward 3 surgery room.

## 8. Content & Sensitivity Notes
- Injury is communicated more through *sound, animation, and consequence* than explicit gore.
- Impaired characters (including Nell, who lives with chronic pain) are portrayed with agency and dignity. Consult disability advisors during writing.
- Include content notes on startup, a reduced-graphic mode, and options to reduce camera sway, blur and tinnitus effects.
